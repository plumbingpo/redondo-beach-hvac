Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in San Diego

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, a central air conditioner usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rising energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and is far less stressful than an emergency swap during the worst heat wave of the year.

If comfort problems do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A weak capacitor is a quick fix. The same capacitor ignored for a season can take the compressor with it and turn a minor repair into a full system replacement. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and miserable nights.

Common HVAC Problems in San Diego

Most local HVAC failures give warning signs before they happen. A breaker that trips every few days, a condenser that hums louder each month, rooms drifting further from the thermostat setting, energy bills creeping up with nothing else changed. These are the early symptoms of failing capacitors, refrigerant leaks, dirty coils, and duct problems, and caught early they're an affordable repair. Left alone, they take out compressors and heat exchangers, the two most expensive parts in the system. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
